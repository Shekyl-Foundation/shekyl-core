- **RPC channel: a client key holds at most four sessions** (`RPC_CHANNEL.md` RT-W11, RT-O37). The daemon's session index counted no sessions per key, so one compromised enrolled key on the operator's network could hold every connection the daemon admits and lock its administrator out of revoking it. `SESSIONS_PER_CLIENT` (4, `shekyl-rpc-channel`, an Assumption: five clients fit in `rpc-connections-max`'s default of 25 with five to spare, and the console's four are its own) is read where a session joins the index, under its lock; the session past it is answered `OverResourceBound`, `bound.sessions`, on its first request, then closed, and the node logs the fingerprint. `CORE_RPC_VERSION` 4.7. The owner-only file rule the daemon's key files follow moves to `shekyl-os-account` (`private_file`), where the tunnel and a CLI on another host will write their keys under it, and the service record's path is composed by one function in `shekyl-rpc-rendezvous` (`service_record_path`). The daemon's channel idle stays one hour. **Reserved slots, and a service's console leg** (RT-O37): the owner's leg and a service's console leg each hold four connections beyond `rpc-connections-max`, drawn only by their own listener when the shared pool is full, so a compromised enrolled host on the operator's network that fills the limit with silent connections cannot lock the administrator out of revoking it. A service listens on the channel at a leg only its account and an administrator can open (Unix: `rpc-console.sock` in the network's data directory, `0600` inside the `0700` data home; Windows: a pipe under a random name with the data home's ACL, named in `rpc-console.pipe`); a connection there that claims any key but the console's is closed at message 3. **The channel's client and the tunnel** (RT-W11): `shekyl-rpc-transport`'s `channel` module dials a node, runs the handshake under its deadline and retry policy, and carries HTTP over the record stream (`ChannelTarget`, `Session`, `Pool`, `ChannelDaemon`), each failure a typed, worded `ChannelFault`. `shekyl-cli` gains `rpc client add|set|remove|list`, `rpc bundle show` and `rpc key create|show`: on another host through `--daemon-bundle-file` and `--rpc-key-file`, on a service's console leg with its console key, or on the owner's leg. The new binary `shekyl-rpc-tunnel` serves a node's RPC to its host's consumers on a loopback listener or a private socket, with a pool of at most four sessions, and ends naming a revocation. Tested end to end against a real daemon: enrol, dial, call, revoke, and a refused call after the revocation.
