- **Daemon: the onion's upload rounds are per descriptor, and the directory check counts silence only on a working fetch path** (`TOR_SUPERVISOR.md` §4.5; Rick, 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16). A service holds two descriptors and Tor uploads each on its own clock, but the round tracker treated both as one round: the next descriptor's upload cut the current one's pass short, and the check's list could hold only the descriptor clients were not asking for. On the floor device's runs of 2026-11-15, 6 of 14 checks counted nothing that way. Each descriptor now has its own rounds. At most two are kept, in the order first seen; at a time-period rotation the one seen first is dropped, which is the one Tor freed. The probe goes out, and its check's list is drawn, once every open round is a minute old. A refusal of one descriptor's uploads is no longer cleared by the other's accepted round. It leaves when that descriptor is dropped, whether or not its round is still open, and when the onion is published again: the key is not accepted after that, and the next publication's own rounds note a refusal again if the directories refuse those. A republish's round that reached nothing waits for the other descriptor's round before the republish is judged failed. A fetch that gets no answer now counts as missing only when more than half of the check's fetches to directories that confirmed the upload were answered; otherwise the silent fetches are not counted and the probe decides. On the control node of the same runs, the probe arrived while every counted fetch was silent, and the check had read that as the onion lost.
- **Daemon: the directory check lists a descriptor's open round once it is a minute old, confirmed or not** (`TOR_SUPERVISOR.md` §4.5; Rick, 2026-11-16). A round is listed once it has a success or is as old as the probe's round wait; otherwise the descriptor's last good round stands. On the floor device's slow node, the descriptor clients ask for had every upload hang and no earlier round, so the check saw only the other descriptor's directories. The node's reachability answer is its own evidence only, never another peer's or an outside service's.
