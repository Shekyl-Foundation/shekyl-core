- **The onion-path check spacing says what it spaces** (Rick, 2026-11-17; `TOR_SUPERVISOR.md` §4.5a, `DAEMON_RELAY_PRIVACY.md` §97 "On-demand check spacing"). `CHECK_SPACING` keeps the starts of checks run on demand 15 minutes apart, not the directory requests. One directory can be asked about the onion again after about `CHECK_SPACING` less `CHECK_LONGEST`, sooner than Tor's own client would ask. Within one check it is sooner still: after a first miss, the second probe asks every directory again. This is accepted because the spacing is an estimate of cost and courtesy, and Tor places no limit of its own on these requests. Comment and documents only; no behaviour changes.
- **Cover follows the encrypted session an observer can time; `CoverClass` is deleted** (`CVR-R2`, `CVR-R3`; Rick, 2026-11-15; `DAEMON_RELAY_PRIVACY.md` §93.2). A carrier channel sends cover when its slot's session is encrypted and its connector declares its timing visible to an observer at this node's end, and on no other condition. A session is encrypted by its connector's native cell or by a layer this node adds to it: the network plan now tells the relay which connectors it wraps in Noise (`NetworkPlan::added_encryption`, `RelayConfig::added_encryption`). Tor sessions now carry cover, the own-origin slot included, and a plaintext clearnet session carries none. `CoverClass` stored the 2026-09-04 ruling that Tor's own volume was its cover as a cell of the connector's column; TRC-1 measured that it is not, and the cell, `cover_class`, `any_open_link`, `OPEN_LINK_COUNT`, `CEILING_ZONES`, `any_link_encrypted` and the `NoiseWithoutOpenLink` refusal are deleted with it. A node builds its carrier over any connectors; its channels carry nothing until a covered session holds a slot.
- **The carrier spends an 8 KiB/s budget on a memoryless cadence** (`CVR-R4`, `CVR-R5`; Rick, 2026-11-15; `DAEMON_RELAY_PRIVACY.md` §97 "Carrier cadence"). The cover budget is the carrier's one stated number, `PER_NODE_BUDGET_BYTES_PER_SEC` = 8 192 B/s per node with no network term, and the mean cadence is derived from it: channels × window ÷ budget, 5 000 ms. The wait before a channel's next window is now one 10 ms tick plus a geometric count of ticks (`CADENCE_TICK_MS`, `MEAN_CADENCE_TICKS`), so the time since a channel's last window says nothing about when its next comes; the bounded `3 333 + U[0, 3 334]` ms law, the 16 KiB/s ceiling and both peak rates are deleted, and a channel's rate is `PER_CHANNEL_BYTES_PER_SEC` (4 096 B/s). A test checks that a channel sends windows faster than the chain confirms transactions at its design capacity (the full-reward zone over the lightest admissible transaction, per block time). The refusal of a carrier whose epoch could not fit a full-size message (`NoiseCannotCrossOneEpoch`) is deleted with the bounded law it relied on. The conformance grades now check the cadence against the geometric law through production draws (`grade_geometric`), the linkage instrument draws its memoryless arm through production, and `utils/carrier/count_windows.py` reports gaps below one tick and each flow's interval mean and spread instead of the old band.
- **An epoch tail keeps every carrier run on the peer it started with** (`CVR-R4`; Rick, 2026-11-18). After an epoch boundary each channel sends `EPOCH_TAIL_WINDOWS` (`MAX_FRAGMENTS − 1`) more windows to the peer it was bound to, then takes its slot's new peer. No message starts during the tail, so a message that started before the boundary finishes on its own peer and a roll never hands a prefix of a transaction to a peer that will not see the rest; with nothing in flight the tail's windows are dummies. The schedule sets the tail without reading the queue, so the switch comes after the same count of windows whether or not a message was in flight. A peer lost during the tail ends it at once, and its half-sent message restarts on the next peer as before. The zone now resolves each due window itself (`Relay::due_noise`, `NoiseDue`), and `Effect::NoiseSend` carries whether a message may start (`Starts`).
- **On a covered session a stem leaves only inside a window, and before its first send this node's own transaction waits for one** (`CVR-R6`; Rick, 2026-11-15, restated 2026-11-18). The zone decides how each stem leaves (`Relay::carrier_to`): through its slot's channel on a session that carries cover, as `RelayCarrier::NoWindow` when such a session holds no slot, and on the ordinary connection only for a session that carries none (a plaintext link, or a node with the carrier off). A relayed stem that cannot get a window — its channel refuses it, or its successor holds no slot — ends its stem phase there (`StemFallback::NoWindow`); the bare-stem fallthrough is deleted. The embargo's reshape goes through the alternate's channel when it holds one, and the carrier's verdict, not the fire, arms its embargo, so an embargo cannot expire while the carrier still holds the transaction. Before its first send this node's own transaction waits for its window: no route, a failed write, a full channel and a window the carrier dropped before any byte left all hold it and plan it again, and none broadcasts it; a node whose peers are all down holds its own transaction, because mining it unbroadcast and publishing the block would attribute it to that node. A node with no connector configured at all still shows its own transaction at once, since it has no network to stem on. The regtest waits for a shown pool are re-derived with the window's wait (50 s, and 90 s on the Tor trio). `Effect::Stem` carries the transaction and its source, and `Effect::Published` is deleted with the last-resort publication it carried.
- **Once sent, this node's own transaction is treated as a relayed one** (Rick, 2026-11-18; `DAEMON_RELAY_PRIVACY.md` §92.5c item 3, which records the reversal). An own transaction that has been sent on a stem draws its embargo from the same random timer as a relayed one, and on fire takes the same two steps: a reshape to the alternate, then the end of its stem phase and a broadcast, an alternate with no window included. The fixed origin retry interval is deleted (`origin_retry_ms`, `origin_retry_secs`, `origin_terms`, `Relay::requeue_origin`): it was a reaction a stem peer could time, by dropping every stem a node sent it and sorting the fixed-time returns from the random ones. `a_dropped_stem_gets_one_reaction_own_or_relayed` fails on any read of "is this one mine" on the embargo path. A window the carrier discards reports whether any of it left (`CarrierOutcome::Discarded { started }`), and only an own transaction of which nothing left is held. The age at which the daemon reports an unpublished own transaction overdue is now status only: the shared timer's 1-in-10 quantile over two fires (`origin_publication_budget_ms`, 7540 s); the submit watchdog's `held_overdue` follows it. A transaction dropped at its first hop is re-stemmed after a mean of 1638 s on Tor (1320 s on clearnet) and broadcast after a second draw. The carrier task serves a due window before its inbox, so a burst of commands does not delay one (`the_carrier_serves_a_due_window_before_its_inbox`).
- **The relay embargo's two live walks run in a nightly leg of their own** (`GATE_LANE=embargo`, `ARMED_EMBARGO` in `run_live_daemon_gates.sh`; a second matrix leg of `nightly-live-daemon-slow.yml`). Each walk now waits for a random embargo fire, so it has no fixed run time and a long draw would have timed the slow lane out. A walk polls for a fire up to the timer's 1-in-1000 quantile (`embargo_fire_wait_secs`), so it calls a fire that does come late at most once in a thousand, and the second walk watches the 1-in-10 quantile for a fire that must not come (`embargo_quiet_wait_secs`). The leg is about 110 runner-minutes of walks at the mean on each branch, derived and not yet measured; the two walks ran 62 minutes in the slow leg on the fixed interval.
- **The carrier keeps its cadence while the relay owner is busy** (`CVR-R6`; Rick, 2026-11-18). On 2026-11-05 a relay owner blocked behind the pool's commit and the carrier, which wrote its windows from that owner's turn, went silent on both channels for 28 s and 32 s, an observable event on the cover link. The carrier's schedule (`Carrier`), each channel's binding and epoch tail, its queue and the session writers it sends on now run in their own task, with their own timing class (`OwnerClass::Carrier`) and their own draws (`RelayParts::carrier_rng`). The relay owner hands it copies — the stem slots and the covered sessions (`Relay::carrier_view`), the epoch boundaries, each message to carry, the session handles — and it hands back what became of each message, a refusal at its door included. A busy owner now delays what it hands the carrier, never a window; a test blocks the owner on the pool for a minute of clock and counts the windows that still reach both stem peers. The driver no longer emits carrier effects (`Effect::NoiseSend` and `Effect::NoiseUnbind` are deleted).
- **The embargo is sized for carrier-paced stems, and a channel's backlog has a simple cap** (`CVR-R5`; Rick, 2026-11-18; `DAEMON_RELAY_PRIVACY.md` §97). Every stem hop now waits for a window, so every embargo is derived with those waits (`derive_embargo_paced`, `EmbargoTimer::shipped`): one window per hop, one more for the share of hops that find a message ahead, and the epoch tail for the share that land in one. That share is a channel's occupancy at the design load: the network's transaction rate at the full-reward zone (`DESIGN_NETWORK_MILLI_TX_PER_SEC`, enforced against the chain's parameters by a test) times the mean stem length, spread over two channels on every node of the smallest network the carrier is sized for (`SMALLEST_NETWORK_NODES`, 100, an Assumption). The embargo mean becomes 1638 s on Tor and 1320 s on clearnet, inside the 30-minute limit (`EMBARGO_LIMIT_SECS`); the Tor embargo would pass the limit below 14 nodes. A transaction dropped mid-stem therefore takes longer to recover than without cover. A channel holds at most one full-size message pending (`BACKLOG_CAP_WINDOWS`, `MAX_FRAGMENTS` windows); beyond it the stem takes `CVR-R6`'s fallbacks. The floor check is restated: a channel's rate, less the tail's share, exceeds its share of the design stem load (occupancy 0.0257). `noise_windows_in_epoch` is deleted.
- **Cover traffic is on by default, with an operator opt-out: `relay-cover`** (`CVR-R1`; Rick, 2026-11-15). The daemon's new public option `relay-cover` (`on` or `off`, default `on`, every network) replaces the development flag `dev-relay-cover`, which is deleted with its staging row (`Staging::RelayCover`) and the plan's `relay_noise`. Cover starts as soon as a stem slot holds an encrypted peer. With `relay-cover off` the node relays no stems — a stem it receives is broadcast at once — its own transactions stem without cover, and it warns on every start what that costs and how to undo it. With cover on, a `p2p-upload-max` below twice the cover budget (16 KiB/s; a stated rule, Rick 2026-11-19) is refused at start and at runtime, by name, with the way out. The budget counts the plaintext the carrier hands to its sessions, and cover is best effort under the limit: a window takes its turn at the link's limiter (`DAEMON_RELAY_PRIVACY.md` §93.2a). Whether the opt-out survives is decided before genesis, with the rest of cover's open questions (`RELEASE_CHECKLIST.md`). Breaking for anyone who passed `--dev-relay-cover` on a test network: remove it.
- **The record layer is held to what cover needs from it** (`CVR-R3`; `P2P_TRANSPORT_LAYER.md`, "What cover needs from the record layer"). A record's size on the wire is a function of its plaintext length only, and rekeying counts dummy bytes like real ones. A new test seals carrier windows holding a transaction on one session and the carrier's dummy on another with the same keys: every pair has one wire length, and the two streams stay on the same key and nonce past a rekey. Two comments in `shekyl-tor` that said every Tor stream crosses "the one guard link" are corrected: TRC-1 measured three guard connections, with conflux spreading a stream over two.
- **The cover documents state the design as built** (`CVR-R1`–`CVR-R10`). `TOR_COVER_POSTURE.md` is restated as the current ruling: cover is the carrier on every encrypted session, the relay is Tor payback, with the pass mark, the known residuals and what reopens before genesis. `COVER_TRAFFIC_RESTORATION.md` is marked complete, its sections kept as the dated record. The user guide, `ANONYMITY_NETWORKS.md` and `TOR_RELAY.md` say that cover is on by default, what it costs, and what it does not hide: it marks a link as running Shekyl; on clearnet a watcher sees which connections are stems and the last stem hop of a broadcast transaction; a transaction of two or more windows completes its stem less often; and a transaction dropped mid-stem takes longer to recover.
