- **The wallet now tells its operator what it is watching, and what went wrong.** `operator alarm` in the CLI (wallet RPC `operator_alarm`) shows every condition the wallet watches for a staker and for every wallet: the staking Tor connection and guard set, the shards a stake must keep, disk space, answering challenges, chain progress, reward claims and reserved funds. Each condition is either checked or "not checked", with the reason. A fault says what is wrong and what to do about it.
- **Some faults outlive their cause.** A guard set drawn anew, shard bytes already discarded and reserved funds spent elsewhere stay listed after the condition clears, until the operator acknowledges each one by its number (`operator alarm acknowledge <incident>`, wallet RPC `operator_alarm_acknowledge`). A fault that is still happening cannot be acknowledged away.
- **A check that stops is never shown as healthy.** If a check stops unexpectedly, its conditions read "not checked" and suggest reopening the wallet. Before this, they could read as fine.
- **The wallet keeps one list of alarms for as long as it is open.** Before this, restarting the staking service could drop a fault the operator had not yet seen.
- A row for whether challengers can reach the persona's onion is in place. It reads "not checked" until the check arrives with the Tor work that follows.
